Look at the Clock Tower rising above the Square of Arms. The tower is generally dated to 1602, when Kotor was part of the Venetian world. Think of a time when most people did not carry a watch. Public clocks helped give structure to the day: opening shops, arranging work and meeting neighbours. In a port town, time was especially valuable. Ships and merchants followed schedules set by weather, tides, work and opportunity. The clock tower offered a common reference in the middle of the city. Notice how the tower sits among buildings from different periods. It isn't simply a monument to time; it is a witness to the changing rhythm of the town. Listen for the sounds of the square before you continue.
