Stand in front of the Cathedral of Saint Tryphon and look carefully at its twin towers. The cathedral was consecrated in 1166, making it one of Kotor's defining medieval monuments. But the building you see today is not an untouched picture of the twelfth century. Its original Romanesque character was changed by earthquakes, repairs and later artistic choices. Some elements reflect the Baroque period; others preserve older ideas in stone. This is the fascinating thing about historic buildings: they carry the marks of damage and recovery as well as design. Think of the skill needed to raise these walls before modern machinery. Then imagine generations of stonemasons returning to care for them. The cathedral is both a building and a long conversation across centuries.
